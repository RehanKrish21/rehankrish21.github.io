## My reading notes

This paper is by my mentor Daniel Severin, and it's about how light can exert a range of powerful biological effects beyond what you actually perceive in vision itself, including things like mood and learning regulation.

I find this is connected with my work with Meta's Try model that maps how the brain associates function with certain visual input, because we've noticed how ipRGCs (which are photosensitive retinal ganglion cells that are used for things like sleeping and the zeitgeber for giving time itself and how you coordinate your circadian rhythm) affect your learning and your mood. This almost begs the question if you can use light input to coordinate learning intervals independent of the SCN's pacemaker function.

Quick note: the SCN is what regulates your circadian rhythm, so mood regulation by light requires an SCN-independent pathway termed the perihabenular nucleus, or the PHB, which is integrated in a distinct circuit with mood-regulating centers and is necessary for driving the effects of light on affective behavior.

This shows that, potentially, what could you do, actually? I forgot what I was going to say, but we can implicate that in this animal model: you can boost learning with light if you adequately correct it. They noticed that innovations to non-SCN targets could lead to learning deficits. By the dysfunction association type of learning principle, you can kind of understand that this independent pathway contributes to mood.

The SCN can also act as a relay for light input to peripheral oscillators, even in the absence of actual molecular clockwork machinery. It can remain responsive to light without having the coordination for light itself. That's kind of interesting too.

I guess ending thoughts are that sleep is good, and these PHB neurons can send collateral projections to places like the ventral striatum and the prefrontal cortex, which is fundamental to our mood regulation. Probably need to sleep to make sure we don't get depressed, right? That's it, thanks.
