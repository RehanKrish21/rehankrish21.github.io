Addendum: Letter to the Editor

Music in My Life

Rehan Krishnan · Johns Hopkins University

To the editor,

Everything else on this site is written the way a paper is written. This one is not, because the subject does not take to it. I have been playing piano for as long as I have been interested in anything, and it was music, not a class, that led me to the science I now work on.

The route was roundabout. I was reading about music and the brain, which led me to work on critical periods, and to the question that still holds me: whether a window that closes in development can be opened again. Musicians are one of the places people look for an answer, because training changes the structure and function of the brain in ways that are measurable and that depend on when the training started. I had been sitting inside that experiment since I was small, without knowing it was one.

There is a version of this I am careful about. It is easy to say that music rewires the brain and leave it there, and the claim is more interesting when it is kept honest: what changes, by how much, measured how, and in whom. I wrote about that carefulness in my essays. Here I only want to say that the interest came first and the rigour came after.

What playing has actually given me is harder to put in a figure. An hour of practice is an hour of being wrong in a specific, correctable way, which turns out to be the same skill as learning to patch a cell. And playing for people who are not listening for mistakes, in the assisted-living homes around Memphis, taught me something about what music is for that no paper has.

Respectfully,
Rehan Krishnan
